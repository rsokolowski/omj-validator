# Model A/B: gemini-3.7-flash (prod) vs gemini-3.8-flash - 2026-09-05

Run with the procedure in [model-ab-playbook.md](model-ab-playbook.md).

## Setup

- Sample: 10 completed prod submissions, stratified over etap and score.
  Covers etap1 scores 0/1/3, etap2 scores 0(->2)/2/5/6, one etap3 (5), one
  `wrong_task`, photos ranging from a clean tablet export to a faint rotated
  phone shot with three pages. Ids: 982d4161 96c8f749 808cf67b ffa34843
  3a2f3f15 c7351cf6 dca1138f bf1aff8c b8e32a36 442afd17.
- Same prompt, same prod Gemini settings (`thinking_level=high`,
  `media_resolution=high`, file cache disabled), same pinned SDK
  (`google-genai==2.19.0`), same API key. Only `GEMINI_MODEL` differs.
- Two passes per model to expose run-to-run noise.
- Ground truth: each submission graded by hand against the official
  solution PDF before reading any model output.

## Feedback quality

Read side by side for all 10. Both models identify the actual gap in every
case and neither leaks the official solution.

- 3.8-flash is more specific: on ffa34843 it names the exact wrong sign and
  shows why the student's case analysis contradicts their own equation; on
  442afd17 it explains why "without loss of generality" is not available
  when `c` is not symmetric with `a`, `b`.
- 3.7-flash pass 2 misread c7351cf6, claiming the student said a positive
  integer cannot end in 0 (the student was talking about the leading digit).
  No such misreads from 3.8-flash in 20 outputs.
- 3.8-flash on 3a2f3f15 calls the student's correct step (3n square => 3 | n)
  a "usterka"; it is not wrong, just phrased as a flaw. Minor.

## Cost and latency

| model | median s | max s | median thinking tokens | max thinking tokens | mean cost / submission |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| gemini-3.7-flash | 19 | 29 | ~2500 | 5500 | $0.018 |
| gemini-3.8-flash | 27-31 | 99-115 | ~5500 | 32000 | $0.041 |

Pricing is identical for both models ($0.75 / $3.75 per 1M through
2026-12-31, then $1.50 / $7.50). The 2.2x cost comes entirely from 3.8-flash
thinking about twice as long, and on two etap1 submissions (ffa34843,
three pages of crossed-out work; 982d4161, rotated faint photo) it thought
for 20-32k tokens and took 73-115 s. That tail reproduced in both passes.
Prod `GEMINI_TIMEOUT` is 180 s, so nothing timed out, but the UI wait
roughly quadruples on those cases.

At current volume (154 completed submissions in nine months) the cost
difference is about $3.50 a year. At the daily global cap of 500 it would be
$11.50 a day.

## Recommendation

Switch prod to gemini-3.8-flash.

- Accuracy: 20/20 vs 18/20 on human ground truth, and the two 3.7-flash
  misses were the ones that matter most for a student (a 0 for real
  progress, a 6 for a proof with a gap). Ten submissions is a small sample,
  so read this as "at least as good, plausibly steadier", not as a measured
  improvement.
- Feedback is a little sharper, with no fabricated observations in 20 reads.
- Cost is 2.2x per submission but immaterial at this product's volume.
- Latency tail is the one real cost. Keep `GEMINI_TIMEOUT=180`; watch the
  `api_time` field in `scoring_meta` for the first weeks. If the p95 climbs
  past ~90 s, consider `GEMINI_THINKING_LEVEL=medium` for 3.8-flash and
  re-run this playbook on the same ten ids.

## Side finding

`GEMINI_MEDIA_RESOLUTION_IMAGES=ultra_high` is set in prod but the provider
logs at startup that `google-genai 2.19.0` does not expose that level and
sends `high` instead. Student images are therefore scored at `high` today.
This experiment ran under the same condition, so it is a fair comparison,
but the setting is misleading and worth either removing or fixing with an
SDK upgrade in its own change.
